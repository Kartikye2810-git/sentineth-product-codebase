# Customer Onboarding Playbook

Owner: Beatrix Ndlovu, Head of Customer Support
Audience: Customer Success, Support, Solutions Engineering
Version: 4.0
Last updated: 19 June 2026

## Purpose

This playbook covers the period from contract signature to the point a
customer is independently productive. It does not cover the sales process
before signature, nor ongoing account management afterwards.

## Onboarding tiers

Enterprise customers receive a named Customer Success Manager, a Solutions
Engineer for the technical setup, and a scheduled onboarding programme.
Business tier customers receive a shared Customer Success queue and a guided
self-service path. Self-service customers receive documentation and in-product
guidance only.

The tier is determined by contract value and is set at signature. Upgrading a
customer's onboarding tier mid-flight requires approval from the Head of
Customer Success.

## The first thirty days

Day zero is contract countersignature. The kickoff call is scheduled within
three working days and must include the customer's technical owner, not only
their commercial contact. An onboarding that begins without the technical
owner present reliably stalls in week two.

By day seven, the customer's workspace exists, their identity provider is
connected, and at least one administrator has signed in. By day fourteen, a
first document set is ingested and the customer has run their own searches. By
day thirty, the customer has at least five weekly active users and has
completed the success criteria review.

## Success criteria

Success criteria are agreed in writing during the kickoff call and recorded on
the account. They must be specific and observable. "Improve knowledge sharing"
is not a success criterion; "the support team answers policy questions without
escalating to Legal" is.

The criteria are reviewed at day thirty, day ninety, and at renewal. Criteria
that turn out to be unmeasurable are replaced rather than quietly dropped.

## Technical setup

Solutions Engineering owns identity provider configuration, initial document
ingestion, and any network configuration. The customer owns decisions about
which document sets to bring first.

Advise customers to start narrow. The most successful onboardings begin with
one team's documents and one clearly defined question type, then expand. The
least successful attempt to ingest everything in week one and then cannot
tell whether poor answers are a content problem or a product problem.

Document ingestion volumes above ten thousand documents in the first month
require advance notice so capacity can be checked.

## Common failure modes

The commercial sponsor leaves and no replacement is identified. Mitigation: a
second stakeholder is identified during kickoff, not later.

Documents are ingested but nobody is told the system is ready. Mitigation: the
launch communication is drafted by Customer Success and reviewed at day
fourteen.

Expectations were set during sales that the product does not meet. Mitigation:
the Solutions Engineer reviews the sales notes before kickoff and raises any
gap immediately, to the account team rather than to the customer.

## Handover to steady state

Onboarding closes at day ninety with a written handover recording what was
configured, what the success criteria are, who the stakeholders are, and what
remains open. The account then moves to the standard account management
cadence.

An onboarding is not closed on schedule if the success criteria have not been
met. It is either extended with a stated reason or escalated.
