# Vendor Selection and Procurement Guidelines

Owner: Priya Raghunathan, Head of Finance
Co-owner for security review: Marcus Bellweather, CISO
Version: 2.1
Effective: 15 May 2026

## 1. When these guidelines apply

These guidelines apply to any commitment of company funds to an external
supplier, including software subscriptions, professional services, contractors
engaged through an agency, and hardware. They apply regardless of whether the
spend is recurring or one-off.

They do not apply to employee expenses, which are governed by the Travel and
Expense Policy, nor to charitable donations, which require Chief Executive
approval individually.

## 2. Spend thresholds and approval

Spend below GBP 1,000 annually may be approved by a department head alone.

Spend between GBP 1,000 and GBP 10,000 annually requires department head
approval and a recorded comparison against at least one alternative supplier.

Spend between GBP 10,000 and GBP 50,000 annually requires Finance approval, a
comparison against at least two alternatives, and a completed security review
where the vendor will process company or customer data.

Spend above GBP 50,000 annually requires Chief Financial Officer approval and,
where the term exceeds two years, Board notification at the next scheduled
meeting.

Splitting a single commitment into multiple smaller purchases to stay below a
threshold is a disciplinary matter.

## 3. Security review

Any vendor that will process, store, or transmit company or customer data must
complete a security review before contract signature, without exception and
regardless of spend level. A free tool that processes customer data requires
the same review as a paid one.

The review is initiated by raising a vendor review request with the vendor's
security documentation attached. Target turnaround is ten working days. Reviews
requiring follow-up questions with the vendor typically take longer, and the
requesting team should plan for four weeks for a vendor handling Restricted
information.

Vendors handling Restricted information must meet the certification bar set
out in the Information Security Policy, must name their subprocessors, must
contractually commit to breach notification within seventy-two hours, and must
provide a deletion guarantee on termination. Procurement does not assess
certificates itself; Security signs that section off.

## 4. Contract terms

Auto-renewal clauses must be flagged to Finance at signature and recorded with
their notice period in the contract register. The most common procurement
failure at this company has been an unwanted auto-renewal caught after the
notice window closed.

Notice periods longer than ninety days require Finance approval.

Price increase caps should be negotiated on any multi-year term. The default
position is that increases are capped at the lower of five per cent or the
consumer price index.

Contracts must specify the governing law and the data processing terms.
Standard contractual clauses are required for any transfer of personal data
outside the United Kingdom or the European Economic Area.

## 5. Data processing agreements

A data processing agreement is required whenever a vendor processes personal
data on the company's behalf. The agreement must be executed before any data
is transferred, not after the service goes live.

The agreement must list the categories of data, the purposes of processing,
the retention period, the subprocessors, and the security measures. A vendor
who will not name their subprocessors cannot be used for personal data.

## 6. Onboarding and offboarding

Once approved, the vendor is added to the contract register with the owner,
the renewal date, the notice period, the annual value, and the security review
expiry. The register is reviewed monthly by Finance.

On termination, the owning team must confirm that data has been deleted, that
access credentials have been revoked, and that any integration has been
disconnected. Offboarding is not complete until the deletion confirmation is
recorded.

## 7. Preferred and prohibited suppliers

The company maintains a preferred supplier list for categories with frequent
purchasing, currently travel, contract engineering, and cloud infrastructure.
Using a preferred supplier removes the requirement for alternative comparisons
but does not remove the approval requirement.

Suppliers on the prohibited list may not be engaged under any circumstances.
The list is maintained by the General Counsel and reflects sanctions exposure,
unresolved security findings, or a prior contractual dispute.

## 8. Escalation

Where a team believes a guideline creates a genuine business obstacle, the
escalation path is to Finance in the first instance and to the Chief Financial
Officer if unresolved. Proceeding without approval and seeking forgiveness
afterwards is not an accepted route and will be treated as a policy breach.
