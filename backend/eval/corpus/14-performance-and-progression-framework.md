# Performance and Progression Framework

Owner: Delphine Okonkwo, Director of People Operations
Applies to: All permanent employees
Version: 1.6
Effective: 1 January 2026

## Purpose

This framework describes how performance is assessed, how progression
decisions are made, and what an employee can expect at each stage. It aims to
make progression predictable rather than discretionary.

## Review cadence

Formal reviews run twice a year, closing at the end of June and the end of
December. Ratings are calibrated across departments before release, so an
individual manager cannot unilaterally set an outcome.

Between formal reviews, managers hold a documented check-in at least monthly.
A formal review should contain no surprises; anything raised for the first
time at a review has already been managed poorly.

## Assessment dimensions

Four dimensions are assessed. Delivery covers what was shipped and its impact.
Craft covers technical or functional quality. Collaboration covers the effect
the person has on others' effectiveness. Direction covers judgement about what
to work on, weighted more heavily at senior levels.

The dimensions are assessed against the expectations of the employee's current
level, not against their colleagues. Ranking employees against one another is
explicitly not used.

## Levels

There are six levels in the individual contributor track and five in the
management track. The tracks are of equal standing, pay equivalently at
equivalent levels, and movement between them is expected rather than unusual.

Progression to the next level requires sustained performance at that level for
approximately two review cycles. Promotion recognises what someone is already
doing, rather than granting permission to start.

## Calibration

Calibration sessions bring together managers across a department with a People
Operations facilitator. Each proposed rating is discussed against written
evidence. Ratings without evidence are not defensible and are revised.

Calibration is not a quota exercise. There is no forced distribution and no
target proportion for any rating.

## Compensation

Compensation reviews follow the December cycle and take effect from 1 April.
Adjustments reflect level, calibrated performance, and position within the
band for the role and location.

Out-of-cycle adjustments are permitted for a promotion, for a material change
in role, or to correct a demonstrated inequity. They require Director-level
and Finance approval.

## Underperformance

Where performance falls below the expectations of the level, the manager
raises it in writing, sets specific and observable objectives, and agrees a
review period of normally eight to twelve weeks with fortnightly check-ins.

Support is expected first: unclear expectations, missing context, and
inadequate onboarding are common causes and are the manager's responsibility
to rule out before concluding that the individual is the problem.

## Appeals

An employee may appeal a rating within ten working days of receiving it. The
appeal is heard by a Director not in the reporting line, with People
Operations present. The outcome is communicated in writing within fifteen
working days.
